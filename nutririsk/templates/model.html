1. Research & Clinical Decision-Support Methodology

End-to-End Pipeline

NutriRisk AI implements an evidence-based clinical decision-support framework designed to stratify malnutrition risk across adult populations (ages 18+) into three triage tiers: Low Risk, Moderate Risk, and High Risk.

Stage I: Data Ingestion & Engineering

Accepts 11 multi-domain clinical inputs (demographics, anthropometrics, dietary diversity, lifestyle, and clinical illness history). Computes Body Mass Index (BMI) deterministically via $w / h^2$ before scaling.

Stage II: Leakage-Free Preprocessing

Enforces strict data leakage prevention by encapsulating median/mode imputation, StandardScaler, and OneHotEncoder inside scikit-learn Pipelines fitted only on training folds.

Stage III: Multi-Class Learning & XAI

Evaluates 5 supervised classification paradigms under 5-fold stratified cross-validation. Employs the champion XGBoost model coupled with dual Explainable AI (Occlusion & SHAP) for decision transparency.

Core Design Principle: The platform operates strictly as a clinical decision-support system (CDSS) for dietitians, healthcare workers, and primary clinicians. It does not replace diagnostic clinical judgment or laboratory assays.

4. Preprocessing Methodology & Feature Engineering

Leakage Prevention
Feature Engineering: Body Mass Index (BMI)

Prior to scaling, somatic height and weight are combined deterministically to formulate Body Mass Index:

$$\text{BMI} = \frac{\text{Weight (kg)}}{\left(\frac{\text{Height (cm)}}{100}\right)^2}$$

Height values are bounded below at 50 cm to prevent zero-division anomalies. WHO standard cutoffs define nutritional triage:

BMI Range (kg/m²) WHO Classification Primary Clinical Implication
< 18.5 Underweight Protein-energy wasting, somatic muscle depletion
18.5 – 24.9 Normal Weight Physiologically balanced somatic reserve
25.0 – 29.9 Overweight Potential micronutrient imbalance / overconsumption
≥ 30.0 Obese Obesity-associated malnutrition & metabolic risk
Data Leakage Prevention Protocol

In medical machine learning, computing global means, medians, or scaling parameters across the entire dataset introduces data leakage, artificially inflating test metrics.

  • Median Imputation: Numeric medians are computed strictly on training folds and applied to test sets without updating parameters.
  • Mode Imputation: Categorical class modes are calculated exclusively on the training split.
  • StandardScaler Normalization: Feature means ($\mu$) and standard deviations ($\sigma$) are fitted on training samples only ($z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$).
  • One-Hot Categorical Encoding: Categorical variables are encoded with handle_unknown='ignore' to ensure runtime safety for previously unobserved categorical states during clinical inference.
Verification: Encapsulated within sklearn.pipeline.Pipeline and ColumnTransformer to guarantee that every cross-validation fold executes an independent fit-transform cycle.

7. Global Permutation Feature Importance

Permutation Scored (Model-Agnostic)
Physiological Interpretation of Top Drivers

Permutation importance scores measure the increase in model prediction error when a feature's values are randomly shuffled. Higher scores denote greater reliance on that factor:

  • Patient Age (16.07%): Strongest predictor; elderly metabolism and vulnerability significantly drive moderate/high risk stratification.
  • Body Mass Index (15.54%): Direct anthropometric indicator of wasting (<18.5) and metabolic disease risk (≥30).
  • Dietary Diversity (13.61%): Micronutrient variety score; low diversity directly indicates dietary deprivation.
  • Recent Infection / Illness (7.19%): Acute catabolism and illness episodes precipitate rapid nutritional deterioration.
  • Household Income (4.98%): Structural socioeconomic determinant of caloric and micronutrient access.

9. Technical Limitations & Ethical Disclosures

Evaluator Guidance
1. Algorithmic Proxy vs. Biochemical Truth

Predictions are generated from reported demographic, lifestyle, and dietary features. They do not incorporate serum albumin, prealbumin, hemoglobin, or total lymphocyte count biomarkers.

2. Synthetic Calibration Constraint

Current weights reflect a simulated benchmark population. Before deployment in a specific geographic clinic or hospital, the pipeline must be calibrated on regional survey microdata.

3. Correlation vs. Causation

Feature attributions reflect statistical associations and predictive dependencies learned by the classifier, not verified biochemical causality or medical etiology.

4. Clinical Triage Function

The platform provides early screening and risk stratification to guide healthcare resource prioritization. It does not issue standalone clinical diagnoses or prescribe therapeutic nutrition.
